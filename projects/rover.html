Autonomous Mobile Rover

A rover built to cross simulated extraterrestrial terrain, find a designated target rock, pick it up, and store it on board, all without a human at the controls. I designed the control electronics and helped integrate every subsystem.

Team
Students for the Exploration and Development of Space (SEDS), UC Irvine

My role

Vision

1The mission

Planetary missions need robots that can work in unknown, unstructured terrain on their own. The challenge was a rover that could reliably cross uneven ground, find a target object, and run a coordinated mechanical pickup, all on limited computing power and a battery.

Our team combined mechanical design, electronics, and embedded software. An Arduino coordinates the drive motors, servos, and sensors, and the chassis and collection mechanism were designed in CAD to balance durability, weight, and maneuverability.

3What I did

Electrical & circuit design

Developed the circuit schematics for the Arduino-based control system, integrating DC motors, motor drivers, sensors, and power management so the rover runs safely and reliably.

System integration

Worked with the mechanical, electrical, and software subteams to bring every subsystem together into a working autonomous rover, troubleshooting mechanical-electrical interface problems and improving overall reliability.
