MedSync Smart Medication Dispenser

A smart pill dispenser that keeps the familiar 7-day organizer and adds motor-driven dispensing, a camera that checks whether the pills were taken, and a caregiver app, without asking the patient to change their routine.

2Why it exists

Problem

Medication non-adherence costs the US healthcare system an estimated $300 billion a year and contributes to about 125,000 preventable deaths. The options today are a plain plastic organizer or a $150–$450 clinical device with no visual confirmation and no remote caregiver control.

Solution

MedSync automates the organizer people already use. A caregiver triggers dispensing from an iOS app, the device turns to the right day's compartment, photographs the collection cup, and checks whether the pills were taken. Missed doses send the caregiver a push notification.

Loading mode: the carousel's 8th slot is blocked. Turning it over the dispensing hole physically seals the hole, so the organizer can be refilled without pills falling through.

4What I did

Electrical & firmware design

Wrote the ESP32-S3 firmware: stepper motor control, NTP timekeeping, BLE communication with the app, image capture and upload, and the loading-mode state machine.

System integration

Tied the firmware, the OpenCV backend, the Supabase pipeline, and the iOS app into one working end-to-end system.

Team credit: MedSync was a five-person team project. I built it with Amogh Thiagarajan, Shashank Sanigepalli, Harsha Ganta, and Soham Belgal. See the team's GitHub repository.
