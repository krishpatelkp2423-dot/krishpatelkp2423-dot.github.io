General-Purpose Digital Oscilloscope

A microcontroller oscilloscope I designed from scratch to answer one question: how much does the analog front end decide what a microcontroller's ADC can actually resolve? One board carries three acquisition paths so I can compare them directly.

1Overview

Problem

A general-purpose scope has to resolve signals that could sit anywhere in a wide voltage range. A fixed-gain front end forces a trade-off: a range wide enough to avoid clipping big signals wastes most of the ADC's resolution on small ones.

Approach

Build three acquisition paths on one board and measure how much accuracy each added level of design complexity buys: a simple direct read, a fully auto-ranging analog front end, and a high-resolution external ADC.

3Inside the auto-ranging path

Step 1 · Lift the signal into the ADC's range

The XIAO's ADC can only read 0 to 3.3 V, but a probed node can swing below ground. The first op-amp stage adds 1.65 V, so a ±1.65 V input becomes a 0–3.3 V signal that oscillates around a 1.65 V axis instead of 0 V. A unity-gain buffer makes that 1.65 V reference from a resistor divider, and the stage stays DC-coupled, because a series capacitor would strip the offset back off.

FIG 3-1 Level shifter, before and afterVout = Vin + 1.65 V
+1.65 Vlevel shifter→

Step 2 · Pick a window, then amplify it

After the shift, the signal goes through a chain of comparators. The first makes sure the voltage isn't too high. The rest form two windows nested around the 1.65 V axis: window 1 is 1.35–1.95 V (±0.30 V) and window 2 is 0.95–2.35 V (±0.70 V). An AND gate turns each pair of comparator outputs into a mux select line, and the mux sends the signal to the gain stage that stretches that window across the full 0–3.3 V ADC range. The comparators use hysteresis so the choice doesn't chatter at a window's edge.

Takeaway: on paper, auto-ranging gives the finest steps for small signals. On the bench, the external 16-bit ADC beat it, and even the plain direct read did better, because the gain-switching stages added delay and amplification errors.

5The math behind it

The board digitizes a probe voltage three ways: V1 and V2 feed the ESP32's 12-bit ADC (V1 through auto-ranging gain stages, V2 directly), and V3 uses a 16-bit ADS8681. Transfer functions, sampling, quantization and a little firmware algebra explain how well each works. Every number below is theory (schematic and datasheet typical values), not a bench measurement.

01

The front end as transfer functions

The Laplace transform turns a circuit into algebra. A capacitor becomes an impedance 1/(sC)1/(sC)1/(sC), and output over input is the transfer function H(s)H(s)H(s). Here sss is a complex frequency: for a steady sine, s=j2πfs = j2\pi fs=j2πf and ∣H∣\lvert H \rvert∣H∣ is the gain at fff. Most of the analog chain is a single pole, the simplest low-pass filter:

H(s)=11+sτ,f−3 dB=12πτH(s) = \frac{1}{1 + s\tau}, \qquad f_{-3\,\text{dB}} = \frac{1}{2\pi\tau}H(s)=1+sτ1​,f−3dB​=2πτ1​

τ\tauτ is the time constant and f−3 dBf_{-3\,\text{dB}}f−3dB​ the bandwidth. A step rises (10–90%) in about 0.35/f−3 dB0.35/f_{-3\,\text{dB}}0.35/f−3dB​.

An op-amp with negative feedback trades gain for bandwidth:

fCL=GBWnoise gainf_\text{CL} = \frac{\text{GBW}}{\text{noise gain}}fCL​=noise gainGBW​

fCLf_\text{CL}fCL​ is the closed-loop bandwidth, GBW the op-amp's gain-bandwidth product, and the noise gain is 1 divided by the fraction of the output fed back to the inverting input. With the TSV794's 50 MHz GBW, the level shifter (noise gain 2) reaches 25 MHz, a 14 ns rise time. An inverting stage of gain −G-G−G has noise gain 1+G1 + G1+G, so the gain stages (gains 5.5 and 2.36, noise gains 6.5 and 3.36) reach 7.7 and 14.9 MHz. The ADS8681's internal filter is far slower: it rolls off at about 15 kHz and delays the signal by about 14 µs.

02

Sampling and aliasing

Sampling keeps one value every T=1/fsT = 1/f_sT=1/fs​ seconds, where fsf_sfs​ is the sample rate. The Nyquist theorem says samples at fs>2Bf_s \gt 2Bfs​>2B fully capture a signal with no content above BBB. A sine above fs/2f_s/2fs​/2 does not vanish. It folds back:

falias=∣f−ℓfs∣f_\text{alias} = \lvert f - \ell f_s \rvertfalias​=∣f−ℓfs​∣

fff is the input frequency and ℓ\ellℓ the whole number that puts ℓfs\ell f_sℓfs​ closest to fff. At the default 10 kS/s, a 12 kHz input shows up as 2 kHz, and the trigger treats it as real.

The ESP32 paths have no filter before the ADC, and their analog bandwidth (several MHz) is 500 to 5000 times fs/2=5 kHzf_s/2 = 5\,\text{kHz}fs​/2=5kHz. Tones and noise far above 5 kHz fold into the trace. The ADS8681's filter is 26 dB down at 100 kHz, so far less wideband noise reaches V3. V3 still aliases at 10 kS/s, though: a 12 kHz tone loses only 2 dB and folds to 2 kHz.

03

Resolution and noise

An NNN-bit ADC rounds each sample to one of 2N2^N2N steps, and the rounding error acts like noise:

LSB=FSR2N,σq=LSB12\text{LSB} = \frac{\text{FSR}}{2^N}, \qquad \sigma_q = \frac{\text{LSB}}{\sqrt{12}}LSB=2NFSR​,σq​=12​LSB​

FSR is the full-scale range, LSB (least significant bit) the step size and σq\sigma_qσq​ the rms quantization noise. The ADS8681's ±2.56 V range (5.12 V full scale) over 2162^{16}216 codes gives 78.125 µV per step. For a full-scale sine, the signal-to-quantization-noise ratio (SQNR) grows about 6 dB per bit:

SQNR=6.02N+1.76 dB\text{SQNR} = 6.02N + 1.76\ \text{dB}SQNR=6.02N+1.76 dB

That is 74.0 dB for 12 bits and 98.1 dB for 16. Independent noise sources add in quadrature (σ12+σ22\sqrt{\sigma_1^2 + \sigma_2^2}σ12​+σ22​​), and averaging MMM samples divides the noise by M\sqrt{M}M​: 16 samples buy 2 extra bits.

Dynamic range is the largest usable sine over the noise floor. Referred to the probe, and taking the ESP32 ADC's own noise as 2 LSB:

Path Step Noise (rms) Dynamic range
V2 direct 1 mV 1.65 mV 55 dB
V1 stage 2 0.42 mV 0.75 mV 62 dB
V1 stage 1 (never selected as drawn) 0.18 mV 0.33 mV 69 dB
V3 ADS8681 78 µV 57 µV 86 dB

A gain stage shrinks signal range and noise alike, so on its own it adds no dynamic range. The V1 figures pair it with V2: V2's largest signal over the stage's lower noise floor. The ADS8681 beats both at every amplitude.

04

The math in the firmware

The firmware undoes the front end with one straight line per path:

Vin=Vadc−VoffgV_\text{in} = \frac{V_\text{adc} - V_\text{off}}{g}Vin​=gVadc​−Voff​​

VadcV_\text{adc}Vadc​ is the reading, VoffV_\text{off}Voff​ the offset and ggg the gain. The inverting stages store signed gains (−5.5-5.5−5.5 and −2.36-2.36−2.36), so the line also undoes the inversion. Calibration takes VoffV_\text{off}Voff​ and ggg from a shorted input and one known voltage. The gain stages instead get a least-squares line fit against V2 over a slow sweep.

Auto-ranging is a little Boolean algebra. Two window comparators set W1=1W_1 = 1W1​=1 inside window 1 and W2=1W_2 = 1W2​=1 inside window 2. A 5k/2k/5k divider on the 3.3 V supply VDDV_{DD}VDD​ puts window 1 at ±VDD/12=±0.275 V\pm V_{DD}/12 = \pm 0.275\,\text{V}±VDD​/12=±0.275V at the probe. Window 2 is ±0.70 V by design. The flags drive the select pins of a CD4052 multiplexer, SA=W1S_A = W_1SA​=W1​ and SB=W2S_B = W_2SB​=W2​, which route the signal into one gain stage:

channel=2SB+SA\text{channel} = 2S_B + S_Achannel=2SB​+SA​

The windows are nested, so a signal inside window 1 sets both flags and selects channel 3, which is unconnected. Stage 1 sits on channel 1, which needs W1=1W_1 = 1W1​=1 with W2=0W_2 = 0W2​=0, and nesting rules that out. As drawn, gain stage 1 can never be selected. Driving SBS_BSB​ with W1⊕W2W_1 \oplus W_2W1​⊕W2​ (one XOR gate), or moving stage 1 to channel 3, fixes it.

6What I did

Analog & circuit design

Designed the level-shifting front end, the window-comparator ladder, the per-window gain stages, and clamp / fault-current protection. Verified matched-resistor gain relationships and power-vs-signal pin separation in simulation before committing to layout.

PCB design & fabrication

Laid out a two-layer board in KiCad. Diagnosed a footprint-library mismatch on the ADS8681 (TSSOP-16), cleared DRC violations (unconnected nets, missing board outline, dangling tracks and vias), verified the Gerber and Excellon export, and hand-soldered the board, including fine-pitch TSSOP-16 and SOIC parts.

Firmware

Wrote an SPI driver for the ADS8681 from scratch, since no Arduino library supports the ADS868x family. Implemented the direct-ADC path and the window-detection and voltage-reconstruction logic that recovers the true input from each sampled window.

Data acquisition & visualization

Built a Python app that streams samples over serial and plots them in real time with LightningChart Python's GPU-accelerated charts.
