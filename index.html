NowNov 2026 – present

Co-packaged optics (CPO) for AI datacenters

Shcherbakov Nanophotonics Lab · UC Irvine

  • Researching how co-packaged optics gets past the limits of copper in AI datacenters: the photonic chip sits in the same package as the switch or processor, so data becomes light a few millimeters from where it is made.
  • Examined the full link, from the SerDes through lasers, modulators, and wavelength-division multiplexing to germanium photodetectors and transimpedance amplifiers.
  • Compared Mach-Zehnder, microring, electro-absorption, and VCSEL modulators, and how each encodes data through intensity (NRZ and PAM4), phase (BPSK and QPSK), QAM, and polarization multiplexing.
  • Evaluated 2D, 2.5D interposer, 3D-stacked, and monolithic integration, weighing fiber coupling losses, thermal drift, and energy per bit.
FIG R-0 Co-packaged optics, transmit sideSame building blocks, new destination
  1. Switch or processorElectrical dataA few millimeters of copper
  2. ModulatorData onto lightLaser light usually comes from outside the package
  3. WaveguideRoute lightAcross the photonic chip
  4. Grating or edge couplerCouple outFrom the chip into a fiber
  5. Optical fiberTo the next rackLow loss over distance

So far: a literature review built on a 2026 Nature Electronics review paper, and a 33-slide technical presentation on how CPO links encode data in light. It builds on my trapped-ion work: the same waveguides, couplers, and modulators, now sending light into a fiber instead of up to an ion.

Integrated quantum photonics for trapped-ion qubits

Shcherbakov Nanophotonics Lab · UC Irvine

  • Studied how the table of free-space optics that steers lasers onto trapped-ion qubits can be replaced by photonic circuits built into the trap chip: on-chip waveguides, grating couplers, and electro-optic modulators.
  • Analyzed grating couplers and waveguide structures for delivering light to the ions, judged on coupling efficiency, mode overlap, and compatibility with ion-trap fabrication.

About

About me

I'm a junior studying electrical engineering in UC Irvine's Henry Samueli School of Engineering, graduating in June 2028. My projects sit where analog circuits meet firmware: level-shifting front ends, PCBs I lay out in KiCad and solder by hand, ESP32 and Arduino firmware, and Python tools that plot what the hardware measures.

On UCI CubeSat I work on systems integration and power, and I'm in charge of the FlatSat test bench. In the Shcherbakov Nanophotonics Lab I research integrated photonics: I started with on-chip light delivery for trapped-ion quantum computing and am now moving into co-packaged optics for AI datacenters. Before UCI, I spent two years doing microbiology and environmental science research in the Discovery magnet program at Spring Valley High School in Columbia, South Carolina.

What I'm working toward

I want to do something innovative: build technology that creates meaningful change. I love creating things out of nothing and watching them develop and get better over time, and I'm always looking for the next project to help build.

My interests run wide, which is why electrical engineering fits me. It's versatile enough to take me from analog circuits and embedded firmware to spacecraft power, photonics, and quantum hardware, and I want to experience as many of those fields as I can and see how much engineering has to offer.

I'm looking for a Summer 2027 internship in hardware, and I'm open to research positions and project teams where I can help build something new.
