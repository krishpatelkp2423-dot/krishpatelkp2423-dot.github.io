NowCurrent project

Integrated quantum photonics for trapped-ion qubits

Shcherbakov Nanophotonics Lab · UC Irvine

  • Explored how photonic hardware can be integrated into quantum computing architectures, focusing on on-chip optical components as a scalable alternative to the free-space laser setups used in trapped-ion systems.
  • Studied how bulky free-space optics can be replaced with chip-scale photonic platforms: microfabricated ion traps paired with integrated photonic circuits for more compact, stable qubit control.
  • Worked with integrated photonic components (on-chip waveguides, grating couplers, and electro-optic modulators) designed to route laser light and deliver it directly to trapped ions with high precision.
  • Analyzed the performance of grating couplers and waveguide structures for delivering photons to trapped-ion qubits, focusing on coupling efficiency, mode overlap, and compatibility with ion-trap fabrication.

2023–24Aug 2023 – May 2024

Using bacteria to control a root-rot fungus

Discovery Magnet Program · Spring Valley High School · Columbia, SC

  • Tested whether the soil bacterium Pseudomonas fluorescens can act as a biocontrol agent against Armillaria mellea, a fungus that causes root rot in trees, shrubs, and grapevines.
  • Grew the fungus on 90 agar plates and treated them with 0 µL (control), 100 µL, or 200 µL of bacterial solution, with 30 plates per group.
  • Built a MATLAB image-analysis pipeline that measured fungal coverage in before-and-after photos of every plate, taken with a fixed camera setup to avoid glare.
  • Found that only the 200 µL group shrank significantly at every image threshold (Kruskal–Wallis and Wilcoxon signed-rank tests), which supported my hypothesis. However, the untreated control plates also shrank, by even more on average, and the final colony sizes of the three groups were not significantly different, so more trials are needed to confirm the effect.
  • Presented at the 2024 South Carolina Junior Academy of Science conference and the 2024 University of South Carolina Region II Science Fair.

2022–23Aug 2022 – May 2023

Cleaning E. coli from water with oyster mushrooms

Discovery Magnet Program · Spring Valley High School · Columbia, SC

  • Tested how well oyster mushroom (Pleurotus ostreatus) mycelium removes E. coli from contaminated water when the fungus is grown on coffee grounds, pine sawdust, or wheat straw.
  • Ran 120 trials (30 per substrate plus a control group): the mycelium grew for three weeks, then sat in E. coli broth for 15 days before I measured the surviving bacteria with serial dilutions and colony counts.
  • Found that coffee grounds and sawdust cut E. coli colonies by about 39% and 33% compared with the control, while wheat straw made no significant difference (one-way ANOVA, F(3, 116) = 45.28, p < 0.0001, with a Tukey post hoc test). This contradicted my hypothesis that wheat straw would work best.
  • Wrote a full research paper and presented it at the 2023 South Carolina Junior Academy of Science conference and the 2023 University of South Carolina Region II Science Fair.

About

About me

I'm a junior studying electrical engineering in UC Irvine's Henry Samueli School of Engineering, graduating in June 2028. My projects sit where analog circuits meet firmware: level-shifting front ends, PCBs I lay out in KiCad and solder by hand, ESP32 and Arduino firmware, and Python tools that plot what the hardware measures.

On UCI CubeSat I work on systems integration and power, and I'm in charge of the FlatSat test bench. In the Shcherbakov Nanophotonics Lab I research integrated photonics for trapped-ion quantum computing. Before UCI, I spent two years doing microbiology and environmental science research in the Discovery magnet program at Spring Valley High School in Columbia, South Carolina.

What I'm working toward

I want to do something innovative: build technology that creates meaningful change. I love creating things out of nothing and watching them develop and get better over time, and I'm always looking for the next project to help build.

My interests run wide, which is why electrical engineering fits me. It's versatile enough to take me from analog circuits and embedded firmware to spacecraft power, photonics, and quantum hardware, and I want to experience as many of those fields as I can and see how much engineering has to offer.

I'm open to internships, research positions, and project teams where I can help build something new.
